What Is Randa? The Moroccan Hand-Stitched Trim on Jellabas Explained

By Khadija · October 1, 2026

If you have ever admired a Moroccan jellaba with a delicate line of stitching running down the front, around the hood and along the sleeves, you have seen Randa. It is one of the quiet details that turns a simple garment into something special.

Randa in simple words

Randa is a decorative edging made by hand-stitching thread along the seams and borders of a garment. It is usually worked in zigzags, waves, small arrows or repeating knots. On a jellaba you will find it on the front opening, the hood, the cuffs and the pockets.

Unlike a printed pattern or a machine border, Randa is built stitch by stitch. That is why two pieces are never exactly alike, and why a well-stitched Randa is such a mark of quality.

Where you can see it

  • Jellabas for women, men and children, mostly along the front and hood.
  • Kaftans, where it often outlines the neckline and sleeves.
  • Home items such as cushions, table runners and scarves.

The materials

Randa is stitched with a fine needle and a strong, smooth thread. Many makers choose a thread colour that contrasts with the fabric, such as cream or gold on a deep burgundy, or a tone-on-tone shade for a subtle look. The fabric matters too. Medium-weight cloth holds the stitches well and keeps the line crisp.

Why it is still done by hand

Hand stitching gives the line a softness and a slight irregularity that machines do not copy. It also lets the maker adapt the pattern to the curve of a hood or the angle of a sleeve. For many families the skill is passed down at home, which is part of what makes it a living tradition.

Can a beginner learn it?

Yes. Randa is built from a handful of basic movements repeated with care. Most beginners can stitch a clean practice sample within a week of short sessions. The key is to start with simple patterns, use the right tools and focus on even tension.
