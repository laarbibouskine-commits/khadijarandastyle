Jellaba vs Djellaba vs Kaftan: What Is the Difference?

By Khadija · October 1, 2026

Moroccan clothing words can be confusing, especially when the same garment is spelled in several ways. Here is a straightforward explanation.

Jellaba and djellaba: same garment

These are two spellings of the same word. Jellaba is common in English, while djellaba comes from French spelling. You may also see jelaba or gallabiya, which is a related word used in other countries. In Morocco it describes a loose, long outer garment with sleeves and, most often, a pointed hood.

What makes a jellaba a jellaba

  • A hood (called a qob), usually pointed.
  • A loose, straight cut that falls to the ankle or shin.
  • A front opening, often finished with buttons and decorative trim such as Randa.
  • Worn over everyday clothes as an outer layer.

The kaftan

A kaftan (or caftan) is usually lighter and has no hood. In Morocco it is a formal, elegant dress, often richly embroidered and worn for weddings and celebrations, sometimes with a belt. In English-speaking countries the word can also mean a casual loose dress.

Quick comparison

  • Hood: jellaba yes, kaftan no.
  • Role: jellaba is an everyday or festive outer layer, kaftan is mainly a dress for occasions.
  • Fabric: jellabas are often wool or heavier crepe for cooler weather, kaftans lean towards flowing, lighter fabrics.

Where Randa fits in

Both garments can carry hand-stitched Randa, but it is most visible on the jellaba, where it follows the hood and front line. Learn more in What Is Randa?.
