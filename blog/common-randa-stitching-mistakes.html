5 Common Randa Stitching Mistakes (and How to Fix Them)

By Khadija · October 1, 2026

Everyone makes mistakes when learning hand embroidery. The good news is that most of them have simple fixes. Here are the five we see most often.

1. Pulling the thread too tight

Sign: the fabric puckers and the line looks wavy.
Fix: let each stitch rest on the fabric instead of pulling it. Think of laying the thread down, not tightening it. Practise on a square sample until the cloth stays flat.

2. Uneven stitch length

Sign: the pattern looks messy even if each stitch is correct.
Fix: draw guide lines with chalk or a removable marker, or mark equal steps with a ruler. After a week, your eye will do the job without help.

3. Thread that is too long

Sign: constant tangles, knots and frayed thread.
Fix: cut a length of about the distance from your fingertips to your elbow. Shorter thread means less tangling and stronger stitches.

4. Starting and ending carelessly

Sign: stitches loosen after washing.
Fix: secure the thread at the start and finish with a small, tidy knot or a few tiny stitches hidden on the back. Always check the back of your work as well as the front.

5. Rushing the corners

Sign: the line is neat on the straight parts but sloppy at turns.
Fix: slow down at corners, shorten your stitches slightly and keep the angle consistent. Practise corners on their own before attempting a full pattern.

Build the habit

Ten to twenty minutes a day beats one long weekend session. The playbook includes a troubleshooting section and a 30-day plan that targets each of these problems.
